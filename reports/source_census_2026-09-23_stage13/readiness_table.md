**Reading key**: "Adapter in pipeline" = a real Python class under `product_tool/adapters/*.py` that `worker.py` actually calls. A research script, a structural probe, or one hand-built card is **not** that — every row below states plainly whether one exists, and none of the brands below LG/Sulpak has one.

## Part 2 — everything else (113 of 127 profiles)

None of the remaining 113 profiles has a real Product page (structural or full-value): `completeness_status` is `product_page_not_found` (majority), `http_blocked` (15 total across the file, including sizeable brands: Gigabyte 362, MSI 400, Acer 354, JBL 159, Logitech 152, AMD 131, Kingston 88, Intel 86, Sony 35, NEC 31, Gorenje 29, GoPro 41, and Dreame's US mirror), `manual_review_required` (8: Electrolux, Gefest, Thermalright, Zotac, EVGA, Don, Logitech support mirror, Gigabyte support mirror), or `javascript_only` (2: `lg_global`, `lg_ru` global mirrors — not the production `lg_kz`/`lg_ru` entries, which are separately confirmed). **0 of these 113 has `production_ready: true`; 0 has a real fixture.**

## Part 3 — priority queue, grouped by confirmed Product-page template shape

Grouping by brand name or storefront-engine guess would hide the real signal. What's actually confirmed, per profile, is which of the 5 layers (discovery / identity / specifications / media / documents) survived a real bounded sample. Profiles sharing the *same* confirmed/partial pattern are the ones where a shared extraction primitive is a real, evidence-backed bet — not a guess. Identity is called out separately per group because it never generalizes as cleanly as specs/media parsing does.

### Group A — discovery + identity + specs + media confirmed; documents the one gap
**Bosch Home (bosch_de/home/uk), HyperX, Cudy** — 3 brands, all one layer short of a complete structural profile.
- **Reusable**: a specs-table extractor and a media/gallery extractor are both real candidates here — all three profiles independently confirmed both layers.
- **Stays separate**: identity. Bosch uses explicit gtin+mpn (strongest signal in the project); HyperX is Shopify-variant-based; Cudy's identity strategy hasn't been characterized in detail (only confirmed as *present*, not documented). Do not assume these three share an identity scheme just because they share a completeness shape.
- **Highest-ROI next step in this whole map**: Bosch already has 3 real structural pages and the strongest identity signal available anywhere — promoting one to a full-value card is the shortest path to a second production adapter after LG.

### Group B — discovery + identity + media confirmed; specifications AND documents both gaps
**Xiaomi/POCO, Asus, DeLonghi, Nintendo** — 4 brands, largest group, two layers short.
- **Reusable**: a media/gallery extractor (all four confirmed).
- **Stays separate**: identity (Xiaomi's own layer notes flag specs as the harder problem here, distinct from Group A) and specs extraction — none of the four has it working yet, so there's no proof a shared specs primitive would even apply across them.
- **Note on scale**: Xiaomi/POCO alone (1744 products) is nearly as large as Samsung; if a specs-layer solution is found for any one of these four, it's worth immediately re-testing against the other three before assuming it generalizes.

### Group C — discovery + identity + documents confirmed; specifications AND media both gaps
**Dreame (dreame_de)** — 1 brand, the only profile anywhere with a confirmed documents layer without a full-value card behind it.
- Not part of any shared-primitive bet yet (n=1) — but if a second brand's documents layer gets confirmed, this is the pattern to compare it against, since it's structurally the opposite of Groups A/B (strong on documents, weak on media).

### Group D — proven full-value cards, but NOT from the structural-layer census; each its own identity strategy
**Samsung (JSON-LD + sitemap), PlayStation (microdata — the only non-JSON-LD identity source in the project), Xbox/Microsoft (image-gallery-centric, had one already-fixed extraction bug), Razer (SAP Commerce/Spartacus SSR-embedded JSON state — currently blocked, not usable)**.
- These four are the closest to being real adapters (each has at least one field-VALUE-level card, not just structure), but they don't share a template with each other or with Groups A–C. Each needs bespoke identity code. HyperX bridges Group A and this group: its structural layers group it with Bosch/Cudy, but it *also* has one real full-value card, same as this group.
- Razer is excluded from "ready to build" status this stage specifically because of the audit in `report.md` §1 — its one card is not valid evidence.

### What this queue is not
Not a recommendation to build 8 adapters in parallel. It's the honest shape of what's already been learned: **Group A (Bosch/HyperX/Cudy) is the shortest path to a second production adapter**, Group D's four brands each need dedicated identity work regardless of shared primitives, Group B is the largest by product count but the least structurally solved, and Razer stays off this list until its host is deliberately un-blocked under policy.
